STAR Dynamics · Validation

What nine self-ratings
show.

Four of the best-known team and personality profiling tools are MBTI, DiSC, the Enneagram and Insights Discovery. Here is what published research says about each, and what we have measured about ours. STAR Assess describes decision-making styles, using the STAR Framework®. STAR Operating System® is the wider system they sit in. STAR Assess is not presented here as a personality test, and it makes no clinical or brain claims.

The nine ratings

A small, self-rated set.

94.8%

9 of 9 rated it 87% or higher (average 94.8%, self-rated).

Nine external users · as of 28 June 2026 · sample standard deviation 4.6 · 95% interval for the mean 91.3 to 98.3

Read this first.

The 94.8% is self-rated. It records how strongly nine people said their own report described them. It is not a test-retest coefficient, and it is not predictive validity. Test-retest, factor structure and predictive validity have not been measured for STAR Assess.

Nine people chose to rate their own report. Agreement with a personal description is often high even when the description could fit many people. That is the Barnum, or Forer, effect [1], so this result is not evidence that STAR Assess measures anything accurately.

In the 28 June 2026 set these nine are called external users. This page does not state how they were recruited, whether anyone declined, or the wording of the question they answered. Early self-rated accuracy is high (n=9). Self-ratings are not evidence of retest reliability or of predicting outcomes. Nine ratings cannot support a conclusion about a wider population.

01 · Published research

What the published research says.

Each row uses a published statistic, with its source. Where this page has no comparable published figure, the cell says so. Nothing here is a score invented for comparison.

Tool What the published evidence says
STAR Assess Self-rated accuracy, as of 28 June 2026: 9 of 9 rated it 87% or higher (average 94.8%, self-rated). Test-retest reliability and predictive validity: not yet measured.
MBTI Published studies of an earlier form report that many people receive a different type on retest after five weeks. Pittenger (1993) writes that as many as 50% were classified into a different type after five weeks, citing Howes and Carskadon (1979) [2, 3]. Pittenger (2005) reports a five-week study in which 50% of participants received a different classification on one or more of the four scales (McCarley and Carskadon, 1983) [4, 5]. These results are not stated here as a property of the current form. The publisher says the five-week figure concerns the obsolete Form G, and reports test-retest correlations of .81 to .86 over 6 to 15 weeks for its current assessment [6]. The instrument classifies people into one of two categories on each of four dimensions. Self-rated agreement comparable with the STAR Assess figure: no comparable data. Predictive validity: no comparable data.
Everything DiSC The publisher, Wiley, reports internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha) of .79–.90 across the eight scales (n=752, median .87) and test-retest correlations of .85–.88 over two weeks (n=599, median .86). These figures are the vendor’s own report [7]. Self-rated agreement comparable with the STAR Assess figure: no comparable data. Predictive validity: no comparable data.
Insights Discovery No comparable data. A British Psychological Society test review of the Insights Discovery Personal Evaluator is sold by the Society and was not opened for this page, so its ratings, date and version are not quoted.
Enneagram A 2021 systematic review of 104 independent samples found mixed evidence of reliability and validity, with partial support for the factor structure and little support for wings or movement between types (Hook et al., 2021) [8]. A single reliability coefficient: no comparable data. Self-rated agreement comparable with the STAR Assess figure: no comparable data.
02 · Self-ratings

Every self-rating landed between 87% and 100%.

Nine people rated the accuracy of their own Cognitive Profile Report. The sum of the nine self-ratings is 853, so the average is 853 ÷ 9 = 94.8% to one decimal place. The sample standard deviation, dividing by eight, is 4.6. The population figure, dividing by nine, is 4.3. A 95% interval for the mean, using the t distribution with 8 degrees of freedom, is 91.3 to 98.3. Teal bars mark the two self-ratings of 100%. Respondents are numbered, and archetypes are not listed beside scores.
